## Key Takeaways

- Most buyers see your home online first, so prep for the photos.
- Small repairs and fresh neutral paint are among the best-value updates.
- Curb appeal matters in photos and at showings.

## Why does prep matter before listing?

First impressions happen online now. Most buyers see your home's photos long before they walk through the door, so a little prep before listing day goes a long way.

## What should I do first?

Declutter and depersonalize. Clear countertops, thin out closets, and put away family photos. Buyers want to picture their own life in the space, and less stuff makes every room feel bigger.

## Should I make repairs before selling?

Handle the small ones. Leaky faucets, sticky doors, and burnt-out bulbs are easy fixes, but buyers notice them. Taking care of them up front can prevent inspection headaches later.

## Is painting worth it before selling?

A fresh coat of neutral paint is one of the most cost-effective updates you can make. It brightens rooms and gives buyers a clean slate.

## How do I improve curb appeal?

Mow the lawn, trim shrubs, add a few potted plants, and make sure the front door and house numbers look sharp. It's the first thing buyers see in photos and in person.

## How can I make my home feel brighter?

Open blinds, clean windows, and swap in bright bulbs. Light, airy rooms photograph better and feel more welcoming at showings.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I renovate my kitchen before selling?

Usually not. Large remodels rarely return their full cost at sale. Focus on repairs, paint, and cleaning, and ask your agent which updates buyers in your area expect.

### Do I need professional photos?

Yes. Professional photography is standard for listings and is how most buyers first judge your home.

### How far ahead should I start preparing?

Start four to six weeks before your target list date so repairs and cleaning aren't rushed.
