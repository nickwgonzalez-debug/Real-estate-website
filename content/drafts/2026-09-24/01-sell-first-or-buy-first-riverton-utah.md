## Key Takeaways

- Selling first gives you a clear budget and a stronger, non-contingent offer on your next Utah home.
- A rent-back lets you stay in your Riverton home for a short time after closing so you only move once.
- Buying first requires qualifying for two payments or using a bridge loan, HELOC, or buy-before-you-sell program.
- A home sale contingency is the weakest option when several buyers want the same Salt Lake County home.

## What are my options when moving up in Riverton, Utah?

Move-up buyers in Riverton, Bluffdale, and the rest of Salt Lake County usually choose one of four paths. You can sell first and then buy, buy first and then sell, make an offer contingent on selling your current home, or use financing such as a bridge loan or HELOC to buy before you sell. Each path trades off cost, convenience, and how competitive your offer looks to a seller. The best path depends on how much equity you have, whether your income can cover two housing payments for a while, and how quickly homes in your target Utah neighborhood are selling.

## Why do many move-up buyers sell first?

Selling first is the financially cleanest way to move up in Riverton. Once your current home closes, you know exactly how much money you have for the next down payment, and you can write an offer with no home sale contingency. Sellers in Salt Lake County generally prefer offers that aren't tied to another sale, so this can help you win the home you want without overpaying. The downside is timing. If you don't find your next home before closing, you may need short-term housing, which can mean two moves and storage costs. A rent-back, covered below, solves much of that problem.

## How does a rent-back work in Utah?

A rent-back, also called a seller leaseback, lets you stay in your home for an agreed period after it closes. You and the buyer set the length, the daily or monthly rent, and who covers utilities and damage in writing as part of the Utah purchase contract. Rent-backs give you time to close on your next home in Riverton or Bluffdale and move directly into it. Not every buyer will agree, especially buyers who need to move in right away, so your agent should ask about the buyer's timing early and include a rent-back in your negotiations.

## When does it make sense to buy first?

Buying first makes sense when you have strong income or savings and the home you want is rare, such as a Bluffdale property with acreage or a single-level home in a specific Riverton neighborhood. Buying first lets you move once, on your schedule, and prepare your current home for sale after it's empty, which often helps it photograph and show better. The risk is carrying two housing payments if your current Salt Lake County home takes longer to sell than expected. Talk with a lender before you shop so you know whether you qualify with both payments counted.

## How do bridge loans and HELOCs help me buy before I sell?

Bridge loans and home equity lines of credit (HELOCs) both use the equity in your current home to fund the down payment on your next one. The table below compares the main options Utah move-up buyers use.

| Option | How it works | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Bridge loan | Short-term loan secured by your current home, repaid when it sells | Higher cost than a standard mortgage and added fees |
| HELOC | Line of credit on your current home, opened before you list | Lenders usually won't open one once the home is listed |
| Buy-before-you-sell program | A company helps you buy first, then sells your old home | Program fees and eligibility rules |
| Sale contingency | Your purchase depends on selling your current home | Weakest offer when sellers have choices |

Rates, fees, and qualification rules vary by lender, so compare quotes from at least two Utah lenders before you commit.

## Will sellers in Salt Lake County accept a home sale contingency?

Sellers in Riverton and Bluffdale may accept a home sale contingency when a home has been on the market for a while or when few buyers are competing for it. When several buyers want the same Salt Lake County home, an offer that depends on selling another house usually loses to one that doesn't. If you plan to use a contingency, make it stronger by listing your current home first, pricing it to sell, and offering a short contingency period. Your agent can check how long similar homes are sitting to judge whether a contingency is realistic.

## How do I decide which path is right for me?

Start with three numbers: your estimated equity after selling costs, the monthly payment you can carry, and how quickly homes like your next one are selling in Riverton or Bluffdale right now. If your equity covers the down payment and homes are moving quickly, selling first with a rent-back is usually strongest. If you can comfortably carry two payments, buying first gives you more control. A local agent and a Utah lender can run both scenarios side by side so you can see the cost of each before you decide. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I buy a new home before selling mine in Utah?

Yes. Utah buyers can buy first by qualifying for both payments, using a bridge loan or HELOC, or using a buy-before-you-sell program. Each option has costs and qualification rules, so compare them with a lender first.

### How long can a rent-back last after closing?

A rent-back lasts as long as you and the buyer agree in writing. Lenders sometimes limit how long a buyer can let the seller stay, so confirm the allowed length with the buyer's lender before you sign.

### Should I list my Riverton home before I find my next one?

Often yes. Listing first shows sellers you're serious, and a rent-back can cover the gap. If your next home is hard to find, start touring early so you're ready to act when your home sells.

### Do I need 20 percent down when moving up?

No. Many loans allow less than 20 percent down, though a smaller down payment may add mortgage insurance. Your equity from your current Salt Lake County home often covers much of the down payment.
