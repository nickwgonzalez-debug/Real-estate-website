How Do I Buy My First Home in Utah?

By Nick Gonzalez, Realtor with Keller Williams · Updated September 22, 2026

Serving Riverton · Bluffdale · Salt Lake County · Utah

Quick Answer

To buy your first home in Utah, get pre-approved with a lender, ask about first-time buyer programs, list your must-haves, tour homes with a local agent, and make a data-backed offer. After acceptance you'll complete inspections and the appraisal before closing. In Salt Lake County, a pre-approval is usually expected before sellers will consider your offer.

Key Takeaways

  • Get pre-approved before touring homes.
  • Ask your lender about Utah first-time buyer programs.
  • Separate must-haves from nice-to-haves so you can move quickly.

Step 1: Why should I get pre-approved first?

Before you start touring homes, talk to a lender and get pre-approved. A pre-approval tells you what you can comfortably afford and shows sellers you're a serious buyer. In a competitive market, many sellers won't consider an offer without one.

Step 2: Are there first-time buyer programs in Utah?

Utah has state and local programs that can help with down payment and closing costs, and many lenders offer low-down-payment loan options. Eligibility depends on income, price, and location, so ask your lender which ones you qualify for.

Step 3: How do I decide what matters most?

Make a short list of must-haves (bedrooms, commute, school district) and nice-to-haves (a big yard, a newer kitchen). Knowing the difference helps you move quickly when the right home comes up.

Step 4: How do I make an offer?

Once you find the one, your agent should pull comparable sales so your offer is grounded in real data. Together you'll decide on price, earnest money, and terms that make your offer stand out.

Step 5: What happens between offer and closing?

After your offer is accepted, you'll have time for inspections and the lender's appraisal. Your agent tracks every contract deadline and walks you through the final numbers before you sign.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do I need for a down payment in Utah?

It depends on the loan. Some programs allow low down payments, while conventional loans often start around 3 to 5 percent. Ask a lender for options that fit you.

How long does it take to buy a home?

From accepted offer to closing typically takes about 30 to 45 days, depending on financing.

Do I pay my buyer's agent?

Compensation is agreed in a written buyer agreement before you tour homes. Sellers sometimes offer to cover it; your agent will explain the options.
